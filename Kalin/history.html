Bulgarian Music History

The history of bulgarian music is long and exciting. It begins with the fusion of three ancient layers: Thracian cults, Slavic traditions and proto-Bulgarian ritual rhythms in the form of folk songs and dances. After centuries of oral transmission from generation to generation, in the middle of the 20th century folklore underwent a revolution - Filip Kutev processed authentic songs into complex polyphonic choirs. This launched the Bulgarian voice onto the world stage, and our mysterious asymmetrical rhythms won Grammy awards and flew into space. Today, traditions are more alive than ever through the ethno-fusion wave - contemporary artists mix authentic singing, kavala and bagpipes with electronic music, trap, rock and jazz, turning our centuries-old heritage into a global modern hit.

In contemporary Bulgarian music, 7 main genre groups are distinguished, in which over 30 specific subgenres are distributed: Pop-folk and Ethno-pop, BG Hip-hop and Urban, BG Pop and R&B, Rock and Alternative music, Electronic music (BG Scene), Authentic and Processed Folklore and Bulgarian Classics and Jazz.

Bulgarian Composers

The first generation of Bulgarian composers includes Pancho Vladigerov, Dobri Hristov, Emanuil Manolov, Petko Staynov, Diko Iliev and others. They laid the foundations of Bulgarian music culture and created a rich repertoire of works that continue to inspire musicians and audiences today. The second generation of Bulgarian composers includes Filip Kutev, Marin Goleminov, Veselin Stoyanov, Alexander Raichev and others. They continued the tradition of their predecessors while introducing new elements and styles into their compositions. Their works are characterized by a unique blend of traditional Bulgarian music with contemporary influences, making them an important part of Bulgaria's musical heritage.
